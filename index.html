Short, practical walkthroughs of the tools that help you find and explain what happened in a movement.

What it does

Bring the video. Find the detail.

Film directly in InForm, open a clip from your phone, or paste a YouTube link. On Android, you can also share straight from the YouTube app. Then take the movement apart, one frame at a time.

Slow it down until you can see it

Step one frame at a time with the arrows, or hold them to walk through the moment. Hold a speed anywhere from 0.25× to 2×. Set two markers and loop the same two seconds twenty times while it sinks in.

Drag your finger away from the scrub bar and it gears down — half speed, a fifth, a twentieth — so you can land on the exact frame with a thumb. Pinch to zoom in on the part that matters.

Six weeks apart, side by side

Put a YouTube performance beside your own attempt, or compare today's take with one from six weeks ago. Play both videos together, loop the matching phrase and step forward a frame at a time. A drift corrector keeps them lined up when the two takes aren’t the same length.

This is the one that changes minds. Students who swear nothing has changed go quiet when today is sitting next to March.

Draw on it. Measure it. Say it out loud.

Pen, line, arrow, ellipse, text and a real angle tool that snaps to square and stays accurate whatever shape the video is. Every drawing has its own visibility window, so a note appears only over the moment it’s about.

Type a note, or record your voice against a moment — the recording sits inside the sentence you wrote. Tap it to hear it and jump the video there.

Why I built it

A tool for the way I train and teach

As a dancer, weight lifter, rock climber and dance teacher, I was always looking for ways to study movement. I wanted a simple but powerful video tool that worked the way I actually trained and taught. I couldn't find the right one, so I built InForm.

The rest of it

All of it is in the same free app, and what is still coming will arrive the same way. There is no second tier.

Record your feedback

Capture the screen while you talk over the video and draw on it, then trim it, crop it, and send the whole review as one clip.

Motion tracking Coming soon

Follow a hand, a barbell or a whole skeleton across the clip, with a trail behind it and live angles at the joints. In testing now, and free like the rest when it lands.

Projects over time

Flip the picture to see a move the way you would face it, drawings and measurements flipping with it. Nothing in the file changes.

Send it to your student

Your videos

Your videos stay yours

InForm has no server or account. It doesn't upload your videos or collect analytics. Your clips, drawings and notes stay on your phone unless you choose to share them. That matters when you teach, especially when you teach children.

  • No account, ever. Open the app and it works.
  • Study your own videos offline. YouTube playback needs a connection.
  • You choose what goes out. Sharing uses your phone’s own share sheet, when you ask it to.

Being straight with you

What it doesn’t do

Worth knowing before you install it rather than after.

  • No cloud library or team accounts. It is a tool on your phone, not a platform.
  • Built for phones. Android and iPhone. There is no tablet layout yet, and nothing runs in a browser.
  • It will not tell you what is wrong. It shows the movement clearly. The judgement stays yours, or your teacher’s.
  • Uninstalling deletes your work. Your phone takes the whole folder with it, so back a project up first.
